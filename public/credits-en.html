Sources and licenses

Many people planted these woods. A little bird wrote down where every piece came from.

Bird photos

Each bird is shown with one real photo. We use the representative image of the species item on Wikidata (hosted on Wikimedia Commons) when it is filed under that species, and otherwise a photo from a research-grade iNaturalist observation (community-agreed identification). Only CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 and public-domain photos with a known photographer are used; paintings, prints, specimens, eggs, nests, maps, birds in zoos or aviaries, chicks and juveniles are excluded.

Each card names the photographer, license and source page; the name links to the source page. Changes: resized, converted to WebP and given a paper frame; not cropped, cut out or flipped. During a fight, a resting bird's photo is shown faded and smaller; in The Woods, photos that don't match the food filter are temporarily dimmed. Framed versions of CC BY-SA photos are offered under the same license.

Birds without a usable photo yet are set aside and cannot be drawn; they show a gray silhouette we drew ourselves.

Bird names and taxonomy

IOC World Bird List v15.2, edited by Frank Gill, David Donsker and Pamela Rasmussen, CC BY 3.0, from the multilingual edition.

Changes: for birds without a Traditional Chinese name, the name was machine-converted from the Simplified Chinese name. The order and family on each card follow the IOC taxonomy; the plain-language descriptions are ours.

Body size and ecological measurements

AVONET: Tobias et al. 2022, Ecology Letters, doi:10.1111/ele.13898, data from figshare, CC BY 4.0.

Changes: attack, defense, type and rarity are derived from the measurements. They are game values, not the original data. “In your hand” uses the Eurasian Tree Sparrow (about 21.4 g), the common sparrow in Taiwan, as the unit of weight. “How it lives” rewrites AVONET's diet categories in plain words.

Conservation status

The category on each card comes from AviList v2025 (AviList Core Team, 2025, CC BY 4.0); birds AviList gives no category for show none. The original assessments come from the IUCN Red List and may lag the latest assessment; the IUCN website is authoritative.

The eBird codes and BirdLife pages behind “Want to know this bird better?” also come from AviList; Wikipedia article titles come from Wikidata (CC0).

The “Endemic to Taiwan” and “Found in Taiwan” labels come from AviList's range descriptions: a range that mentions only Taiwan counts as endemic, and one that mentions Taiwan counts as found there. Many ranges only say something broad like “eastern Asia”, so a bird without a label may still occur in Taiwan.

The category is one of the factors used to calculate rarity. Rarity was fixed when the card pool was built, so later category updates do not change a card's rarity.
