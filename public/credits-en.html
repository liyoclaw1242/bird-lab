Sources and licenses

This game stands on the work of many people. Below is the source and license of each asset, and what we changed.

Bird stickers

Bird stickers: [removed], drawn by [removed] ([removed]), used under CC BY 4.0. The stickers are illustrations, not identification references.

Changes: the sticker files themselves are unmodified. The in-game line art is generated automatically from the sticker outlines and is also offered under CC BY 4.0.

Most stickers come from the original author's version 2.2.3 (2026-11-05). A few species were renamed or lumped as subspecies when the original author moved to the AviList taxonomy, so the new version has no file under the old name; those still use the version 0.1 sticker (2026-10-30), also under CC BY 4.0.

Each sticker was drawn from a reference photo. The sources and licenses of the reference photos are recorded in the original author's data file. The reference photos themselves are not part of this game.

Bird names and taxonomy

IOC World Bird List v15.2, edited by Frank Gill, David Donsker and Pamela Rasmussen, CC BY 3.0. A few English names use the newer names from AviList v2026 (AviList Core Team, 2026, CC BY 4.0). Changes: for birds without a Traditional Chinese name, the name was machine-converted from the Simplified Chinese name.

Body size and ecological measurements

AVONET: Tobias et al. 2022, Ecology Letters, doi:10.1111/ele.13898, CC BY 4.0. Changes: attack, defense, type and rarity are derived from the measurements. They are game values, not the original data.

Fun facts on the cards

The “First impression” and “Did you know” notes come from the Chinese data in [removed], rewritten by the original author from Chinese Wikipedia and other sources, CC BY-SA 4.0. Changes: machine-converted to Traditional Chinese. The Traditional Chinese version is likewise offered under CC BY-SA 4.0. The “source” link on a card opens the main Wikipedia article it draws on. These notes appear only in the Chinese version of the game.

Conservation status

The category on each card is taken from AviList v2026 (BirdLife v10, CC BY 4.0), falling back to Wikidata (CC0) where AviList gives none. The original assessments come from the IUCN Red List and may lag the latest assessment; the IUCN website is authoritative. The category is also one of the factors used to calculate rarity. Rarity was fixed when the card pool was built, so later category updates do not change a card's rarity.
